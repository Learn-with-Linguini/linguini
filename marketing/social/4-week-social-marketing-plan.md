# Linguini: four-week social marketing plan

This plan covers **X, TikTok, LinkedIn and Instagram** for Linguini's public beta. It is written around the product that exists today:

- Photo-led lessons from a learner's own photos or ready-made scenes.
- Learner review of AI-suggested objects before a lesson begins.
- Word cards, sentence activities and two-way I-Spy.
- A photo journal, vocabulary progress, XP and farfalle streaks.
- Unlimited photo lessons during the public beta, with no payment card required.
- The learner app supports Spanish, French and German, plus beginner Korean, from English.
- The no-signup website demo currently showcases Spanish and French.

Review the beta pricing and language limits before reusing this plan after the beta.

## Campaign objective

The four weeks should move people through four questions:

1. **What is Linguini?** A language lesson made from a photo of your day.
2. **Can I trust it?** AI suggests vocabulary, but the learner reviews and controls it.
3. **Will I enjoy returning?** I-Spy, journal pages, XP and pasta streaks turn practice into a habit.
4. **Why should I try it now?** Public-beta users receive unlimited photo lessons and can influence what is built next.

The primary CTA is **Try the public beta**. The secondary CTA is **Tell us what you would photograph or improve**.

# LinkedIn plan

## Cadence

- **Tuesday:** Product insight or build-in-public story.
- **Thursday:** Demonstration, beta learning or specific feedback request.
- Prefer one clear idea and one question over a long feature list.
- Use three to five relevant hashtags.

## Week 1: Why start with a photo?

### Tuesday — Product premise

> Vocabulary lists are easy to forget because they have no place attached to them.
>
> Linguini starts somewhere different: a photo from the learner's own day. The app finds possible objects, the learner chooses the useful ones, and those words become a short practice session and journal page.
>
> The product bet is simple: a word connected to a familiar place may be easier to retrieve than a word encountered in isolation.
>
> If you were testing that assumption, what behaviour would you measure first?
>
> #LanguageLearning #EdTech #ProductDevelopment #BuildInPublic

### Thursday — Product walkthrough

> A Linguini session follows one repeatable trail:
>
> 1. Capture or choose a photo  
> 2. Review the objects found in it  
> 3. Learn the confirmed words  
> 4. Practise through sentences and two-way I-Spy  
> 5. Keep the day as a journal page
>
> We designed the loop to fit into an ordinary day rather than compete with it.
>
> Public beta: [TRACKED LINK]

## Week 2: Human control in an AI feature

### Tuesday — AI design principle

> Image recognition is useful, but it is not a trustworthy curriculum by itself.
>
> In Linguini, the output of photo analysis is presented as a draft. Learners can remove a wrong object, add something the model missed and choose what is worth practising before a lesson is generated.
>
> That review step serves two purposes: it prevents avoidable errors and gives the learner ownership over the lesson.
>
> Where else should an AI-generated draft require explicit human confirmation?
>
> #ResponsibleAI #ProductDesign #EdTech #HumanInTheLoop

### Thursday — Honest beta question

> One of the most useful things a beta product can do is make reporting friction easy.
>
> Linguini lets learners report an incorrect solution from inside a lesson, where the report can retain the relevant lesson context. Longer questions can go through the public Contact Us page.
>
> We are interested in the moment before a report too: what makes someone abandon a confusing answer instead of telling the team?
>
> #UserFeedback #ProductManagement #BuildInPublic

## Week 3: The habit loop

### Tuesday — From practice to memory

> The lesson is not meant to end when the last answer is checked.
>
> Linguini carries the photo and new vocabulary into a journal page, giving learners a reason to use the words in their own writing and a place to find them again later.
>
> Progress, XP and a farfalle-shaped streak support the habit, but the journal is the durable record: not just what was studied, but where it came from.
>
> What has helped you return to a learning product after the novelty wore off?
>
> #LearningDesign #HabitFormation #EdTech #LanguageLearning

### Thursday — I-Spy interaction

> Recognition and production are different skills.
>
> That is why Linguini's I-Spy has two phases. First the learner interprets a clue and identifies an object. Then the learner writes a clue and asks the app to identify what they meant.
>
> The same photo supports both directions of practice without introducing an unrelated exercise context.
>
> #LearningExperience #LanguagePractice #ProductDesign

# Community and response guidelines

- Reply to specific feedback with a specific acknowledgement. Avoid a generic “Thanks!” when someone has identified a real problem.
- Never claim learner counts, retention, reviews or outcomes that have not been measured.
- Do not promise that AI analysis is always correct. The review step is part of the product story.
- Say **beginner Korean** until broader Korean learning paths are implemented and verified.
- Distinguish the four-language learner app from the Spanish/French interactive website demo.
- Ask permission before reposting a learner's photo, journal page, message or username.
- Move account-specific support and personal information into the Contact Us flow rather than resolving it in public comments.

# Weekly review

At the end of each week, record:

- Reach or impressions by platform.
- Profile visits.
- Link clicks using UTM data.
- Signups attributed to each platform, if signup attribution is available.
- Saves and shares on educational posts.
- Meaningful comments, questions and reported confusion.
- Which opening line retained attention best on TikTok and Reels.

Keep the next week's strongest theme, revise one weak format and avoid changing the entire plan based on a single post.
