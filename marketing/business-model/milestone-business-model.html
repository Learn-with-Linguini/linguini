Milestone 6: Come up with a monetisation and pricing strategy

Linguini is freemium. The free plan limits the one thing that costs us money, AI analysing a learner’s own photo, and keeps the full learning loop free.

For the full cost model, interactive charts and sources, see our blog post: linguini-landing.vercel.app/blog/linguini-business-model.

TierPriceFeatures
Free$01 uploaded scene a day, unlimited preloaded scenes, the full loop (vocabulary review, I Spy, object description, feedback, saved progress), 1 journal page a day
Plus$7.99 a month or $49.99 a yearUp to 10 uploaded scenes a day, speaking feedback, review mode, 10 photos per journal page

Speaking feedback and review mode are future features from Milestone 5, so we will only charge for Plus once they ship.

Why this suits our users

  • Most learners never pay: only 2.3% of education-app downloads convert to paid (RevenueCat, 2026), and many of our users are students. The free plan must be useful on its own and cheap for us to give away.
  • They learn in short moments: one uploaded scene a day builds a daily habit without interrupting a lesson with a paywall.
  • The upgrade moment is clear: a learner who wants more than one photo a day, on a trip or in a new city, is getting the most value, so that is when we ask them to pay.

Factors behind the price

FactorWhat we found
AI inference costOne uploaded scene costs about 2.0¢ with the models we chose after testing them on our own prompts (all called through OpenRouter). Learning tasks are half of that and scene analysis over a third. The cheapest models our app accepted bring it to 0.8¢, but they score lower today. Preloaded scenes are analysed once, so replaying one costs about 0.2¢.
Other costsHosting is a fixed $52–70 a month. Stripe keeps 3.4% + 50¢ per payment, so we push the annual plan. After fees, a Plus learner brings in about $5.24 a month.
CompetitionCapWords charges $20–30 a year but stops at flashcards. Duolingo ($84–96), HelloTalk ($79.99) and Babbel ($108) charge more. Anki is free apart from a $24.99 iPhone app, but learners make their own cards. We sit between the two groups.
Perceived valueThe closest alternative to describing your surroundings with feedback is a tutor at $15–60 an hour (italki). A year of Linguini should cost less than one or two lessons.
Our stageBefore launch, learners and retention matter more than margin. Hence the free core loop, the 48% annual discount and Founding Plus, which trades margin for early payers who tell us what to build.

Cost sets the floor (about $15 a year per Plus learner), competitors set the range, and a tutor sets the ceiling. $49.99 a year sits close to the $44.99 education-app median.

How costs scale with usage

Because AI is paid per scene, costs grow with use, not with sign-ups. A free learner who uses their scene every day costs $0.64 a month; a typical Plus learner costs $0.74 against $5.24 of revenue. Only a Plus learner using all ten scenes every day loses money ($7.13 a month), which is why Plus is capped at ten rather than unlimited.

The real cost is the free learners each payer supports: about 42 at 2.3% conversion. On the chosen models, Linguini breaks even when about 4% of learners pay, so our target is 4.5%, enough to cover AI and hosting from 5,000 learners a month. With the cheapest models it would break even at about 1.6%, but they need to pass our tests first. Before a wide launch we will fix the lesson format problem that stops a cheaper lesson model from passing, retest the cheaper models, enforce the daily limits on the server and track the cost of every AI call.

Why freemium

ModelDecision
Freemium with daily limitsChosen. Learners try the whole loop, and the limit caps what a free learner costs.
Usage-based creditsRejected. It matches cost exactly, but counting credits discourages a daily habit.
Flat subscriptionRejected. Students will not pay before trying.
Lifetime dealRejected. AI costs continue after the one payment.
AdvertisingRejected. It earns little at our size, and ads next to personal photos would hurt trust.

Revenue streams

  • Now: Plus and Founding Plus subscriptions, sold on the web to avoid app-store fees.
  • Later: a family plan (about $79.99 a year for 4), classroom licences and printed journals.
