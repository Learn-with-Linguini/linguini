# Linguini 50-second explainer: production script

**Working draft · 24 September 2026.** This is the brief for the 2D flat-vector animated explainer, and the source of truth for the rendered cut in the [media kit](README.md). The video is animated artwork, not a screen recording of the product, so the YouTube and Product Hunt descriptions should say it is an animated explainer. For a screen recording of the live demo, use the 35–45 s storyboard in [listing.md](../product-hunt/listing.md#demo-video-storyboard-3545-seconds).

**Positioning:** *The world is your lesson.* Linguini turns a photo of your day into Spanish or French words, quick speaking and I-Spy practice, and a journal page.
**Audience:** Beginner and returning learners who want useful words from familiar places and a short daily habit.
**Three features:** (1) words found in your photo, with articles attached and you choosing which to keep; (2) hear it and say it, plus two-way I-Spy; (3) the journal, backed by streaks and a pasta avatar.

**Rendered cut:** `export/explainer.mp4` (1080p, 24 fps, 49.5 s, voiceover, music and sound effects), captions in `export/explainer.srt`, a poster frame and three style frames, all produced by [`explainer.py`](explainer.py). It uses the site's real photos, brand assets and the *Across the bay* scene content from `landing/data/scenes.ts`, so the vocabulary, the I-Spy clue and the journal text match the live demo. A studio can use it as the timing reference and replace the photos and simple shapes with finished vector artwork.

## Visual system

- **Background:** deep ink `#263238`, plus a darker herb-teal (about `#17403F`) made only for the video. This takes the brand's text colors and turns them into dark canvases to match the high-contrast brief.
- **Accents:** tomato `#E85D32` for actions and clicks, pasta `#F9B233` for discoveries and rewards, teal `#2E9C99` for correct answers, and butter `#FFF9ED` / cream `#FFF1D2` for app cards and panels.
- **Type:** Baloo 2 800 for headlines, Nunito Sans 800 for UI. Sentence case throughout. The supplied wordmark is the only expressive lettering.
- **Linking device:** one continuous pasta-yellow **linguine strand**. The camera follows it through every pan instead of cutting, and it ends as the underline of the wordmark. This follows the "noodle motion" rule in [`frontend/design.md`](../../frontend/design.md).
- **Burst kit:** tomato and teal rings, 6–8 radial speed lines, and pasta-shaped confetti (farfalle, fusilli, penne, macaroni).
- **Outlines:** dark-teal (`#21716F`) contours, 2 px at 1080p, with slightly imperfect hand-drawn geometry. No thick black cartoon outlines.
- **Easing:** elements enter with overshoot, `cubic-bezier(.34,1.56,.64,1)`, scaling 0 → 110% → 100% over 8–10 frames. Camera moves use `cubic-bezier(.65,0,.35,1)`. Bursts last 10–12 frames at 24 fps.

## Audio and delivery

- **Voice:** Kokoro-82M `af_heart` (warm, natural American female; Apache-2.0 model, rendered locally, cleared for commercial use) at 1.05× speed. Each sentence is placed on its visual beat, and every line ends before the next begins. For a human voice actor, the timings in the table are the slots to hit.
- **Music:** an original 120 BPM track in D major (I–V–vi–IV), synthesised in [`soundtrack.py`](soundtrack.py), so there is no licence to clear. Plucked arpeggios and a pad play under the hook. Kick, clap, hats and bass drop in at 0:06.5 on the logo reveal. A two-bar breakdown sits under "Hear it… then say it", and a crash with a glockenspiel hook lands on the end card at 0:44.5. Every scene cut sits on a beat. The music ducks about 8 dB under the voice.
- **Sound effects:** a pop for every shape burst, with a heavier thump on the big ones, and a whoosh for every scene transition. They are placed at the exact frames where the renderer draws them.
- **Loudness:** normalised to −14 LUFS with a −1.5 dBTP ceiling, the YouTube reference.
- **Masters:** 1920 × 1080, 24 fps, 16:9 for YouTube and Product Hunt (Product Hunt takes a public or unlisted YouTube URL). Reframe 0:14.5–0:36 for 1:1 and 9:16 cut-downs.
- **Accessibility:** no captions are burned in. Upload `explainer.srt` to YouTube as closed captions. Keep each Spanish word and its English gloss on screen for at least 1.5 s. Avoid full-frame flashes: the shutter flash stays inside the phone frame.
