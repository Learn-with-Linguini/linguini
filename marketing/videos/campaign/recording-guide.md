# Human narrator and shoot guide

Use this with [the three campaign scripts](scripts.md). The deliverable is a film with a real person’s voice and believable physical detail. The voice may belong to the person on camera, but the camera never needs to show them delivering lines directly. Record the scripts as original performances; do not clone an actor, imitate the Zoom reference, or reuse any of its media.

## Cast and voice

Choose one adult narrator with a natural, present voice. A bilingual Spanish/French speaker is ideal but not required; coach the two words with a fluent speaker and listen back. Cast one on-camera learner, or let the narrator play that part. The learner is an actor discovering the **curated landing demo**, not a testimonial customer. Avoid stock “smiling at phone” acting: ask them to notice a detail, make a choice, and react subtly to getting a sentence right.

For auditions, record these three lines in one take: “The world is already speaking”; “Give it a place”; “Your first words start here. Try Linguini.” Listen for a believable shift from intimate attention to conviction. Favour a voice with texture and pauses over a polished announcer read. Do not process a synthetic voice to imitate a human performer.

Pronunciation check: Spanish **el coche** is roughly “el KOH-cheh” (the demo IPA is `/ˈkotʃe/` for *coche*). French **la voiture** is approximately “lah vwah-TÜR”; the demo IPA is `/vwatyʁ/` for *voiture*. These English hints are only for rehearsal. Have a fluent speaker approve the final spoken takes and keep the article attached to the noun.

## Voice session

Record dry voice in a quiet, treated room at **48 kHz / 24-bit mono WAV**. Put a pop filter 15–20 cm from the microphone; keep the distance fixed. Aim for peaks around −12 to −6 dBFS with no clipping. Capture ten seconds of room tone. Record three complete takes of each concept, then pickups of the hook, foreign words and final CTA. Read the exact timed line as written; the edit can preserve a real breath between ideas. Leave one second before and after each take so the editor has handles.

Direct the 30-second read from a near-whispered observation into a grounded invitation. Let **el coche** and **la voiture** land cleanly. The 20-second read starts with a trace of frustration, turns at “Give it a place,” and ends with controlled resolve. The 15-second read has a delighted first discovery, then a crisp final invitation. None should sound like a trailer, a product tutorial or a list of features. Prefer a human pause and a cut over stretching or heavily speeding a line to meet a timestamp.

Keep voice and music as separate tracks. Duck music under the two foreign-language words and the CTA; leave footsteps, card flicks, taps and the page turn quiet enough that every consonant remains clear. Mix final dialogue to a consistent level and check intelligibility on phone speakers. Supply a clean voice WAV, final mix WAV, final MP4 and sentence-accurate SRT. The existing synthetic voice in `../launch-film/voice/voice.wav` is a timing reference only for these new cuts.

## Realistic shoot list

| Setup | Capture | Script use | Practical note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Street or outdoor walkway | Person slows, looks toward a detail; close-ups of eyes, shoes, light on a wall. | A, B ending | Shoot in a controllable place. Do not imply a camera upload or a matched new-photo analysis. |
| Quiet table | A few plain paper vocabulary cards, an ordinary pen, hand setting down a forgotten card. | B | Avoid another product’s interface or logo. Photograph the card front and reverse for matching cuts. |
| Phone with Linguini landing demo | Over-shoulder and close phone capture of choosing the **hillside-street** ready-made scene. | All three | Set phone brightness, lock notifications, and clear personal data. Use a screen capture as a clean replacement if moiré appears. |
| Demo capture | Spanish/French car card, selectable words, Spanish I-Spy answer, gap task, sentence builder, sample journal. | All three | Capture the real live landing flow at `?photo=hillside-street#try`; allow UI states to settle, then cut for pace. Keep **SAMPLE JOURNAL** on stylised journal shots. |
| Human reaction | One small breath, a nod or a glance up after the completed sentence. | A, B, C ending | Direct a genuine moment, not a broad celebratory grin. |
| End card | Wordmark, mascot, `linguini-landing.vercel.app`, and **Try the mini session**. | All cuts | Hold URL and CTA at least two seconds; keep inside mobile safe area. |

Shoot live action in 4K at a consistent frame rate, then finish the horizontal master at 1920 × 1080. Compose the learner slightly off centre so the same take can crop to 9:16. Lock exposure and white balance for each setup. Record a few seconds longer than the edit window for every action. Capture 10–15 seconds each of street ambience, room tone, a paper flick, a finger tap and a page turn; these give the edit physical texture without borrowed audio.

For the phone sequence, show a scene **chosen from the site** before showing its prepared word markers. If the team later captures a genuine user-photo analysis workflow, make that a separate full-product film after it has been verified in production. The campaign scripts above are ready for the current curated demo and do not need that workflow.

## Edit and approval pass

1. Lay down the human voice first and time each sentence to the script table. Cut picture to the line’s idea; let one recognisable action read before the next cut.
2. Bring music from almost bare ambience to a decisive end-card lift. Use an original or properly licensed track. Create fresh sound effects or record your own; no music, footage, dialogue or likeness from the Zoom reference.
3. Overlay only short readable headlines. Burn in complete captions from the final human take; caption the Spanish and French articles correctly. Keep captions clear of phone UI, the subject’s face and platform controls.
4. Review at full size and on a phone with sound, then muted. Verify the URL, spoken words, exact duration, caption sync, correct language toggle, and that the sample journal is recognisably a demo page. Have the team verify photo/performer permissions before publication.

[Zoom’s published campaign description](https://news.zoom.com/zoom-ahead-brand-campaign/) supports only the broad cue of a dramatic human story driven by a relatable friction point. Treat it as a mood reference, not a shot list or performance template.
