Milestone: Create a landing page for marketing purposes

Our landing page explains why Linguini exists, shows what it does and asks visitors to start learning for free. It is a separate Next.js site on Vercel, so we can change marketing copy and SEO without touching the learner app. Pages are rendered on the server, so search engines and link-preview bots get the full page without running JavaScript.

Live site: linguini-landing.vercel.app. Try sharing it in Telegram, WhatsApp or LinkedIn to see the preview card.

Hero, features and pricing

  • Show, don’t tell: instead of a screenshot, the hero is the product. Visitors pick a photo and play a mini lesson: word cards, I-Spy, a fill-in-the-blank and a sentence builder, ending with a journal page. The value is clear before anyone signs up.
  • One clear action: “Start learning free” appears in the hero, the pricing section, the closing section and on every blog post. “Try it on a photo” is the lower-commitment option beside it.
  • Features you can touch: each feature tile works, so you can hear a phrase, keep or drop a word, or pick a pasta avatar, instead of reading a bullet list.
  • Pricing on the page: learners compare prices before signing up, so we show them and link to our business model post to explain them. All prices come from one file, so the page, the search-engine data and our AI summary can never disagree.

Search engine optimisation

  • Semantic HTML: one h1 per page, every section labelled by its h2, and lang="es" and lang="fr" on Spanish and French words.
  • Titles and descriptions: every page has its own title (“Page · Linguini”), a description of about 160 characters so search results don’t cut it off, and a canonical URL.
  • Crawling: robots.txt allows everything and points to sitemap.xml, which lists every page and blog post with its date.
  • Structured data: Organization, WebSite, SoftwareApplication (with our three price offers), FAQPage, BlogPosting, and breadcrumbs and a vocabulary list on the language pages. This makes pages eligible for rich results.
  • Speed and accessibility: self-hosted fonts, AVIF/WebP images, alt text on every photo, a skip link and reduced-motion support.

Social sharing previews

Every page has its own Open Graph and X tags: title, description, URL and a large image with alt text. We generate each image with Vercel’s next/og from our real fonts, photos and pasta mascots, so previews stay on-brand without anyone designing them by hand.

Being found by AI assistants

More people now ask ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google’s AI Overviews than search by keyword, so we optimised for these generative engines too (GEO):

  • llms.txt: a plain-text summary of what Linguini is, how it works, prices, FAQ and links, built from the same data as the page so it never goes out of date.
  • Pages that answer real questions: “Learn Spanish with photos” and “Learn French with photos” each list 40 real words with article, gender and pronunciation, plus a worked five-minute lesson. A comparison post covers Linguini, Duolingo, Babbel, Speak and CapWords, using only prices we sourced for our business model, and says when Linguini is the wrong pick.
  • Quotable answers: the FAQ and vocabulary are marked up so assistants can quote them directly.

Decisions we made

OptionDecision
Separate marketing siteChosen. It deploys on its own and serves complete HTML to crawlers. Putting it inside the learner app would tie marketing changes to app releases.
Plain share linksChosen over the Facebook and X widgets. No third-party scripts means no tracking cookies, a faster page and no cookie banner.
Generated preview imagesChosen over hand-made ones. Every new page and blog post gets an on-brand card automatically.
Blocking share pages in robots.txtRejected. It would also block the link-preview bots. We hide them from search with noindex instead.
Social login (Google, GitHub)Deferred. It belongs in the learner app, not the landing page, and is next on our list for lowering sign-up friction.
Pages in other languagesDeferred. Linguini only teaches from English, so translated pages would promise something the app can’t do yet.
TestimonialsDeferred until we have real ones. We will not invent any.

What our audit found and fixed

We checked the live site the way social media bots read it.

ProblemFix
The blog and credits pages showed the home page’s title in their previewA shared helper now gives every page its own complete set of tags
Blog posts had no preview image, so they showed as bare linksA generated card for each post
The home page description was 180 characters and got cut off in search resultsShortened to 160
Prices were copied in three placesOne source for the page, the structured data and llms.txt

A script now crawls the sitemap and fails if any page is missing a title, description, canonical URL, single h1, preview tag, working preview image or valid structured data. We run it before each release.

Next steps for reach

The site is already set up for each of these. Each needs an account or a purchase, and then one setting.

  • Custom domain: the site switches to it automatically and permanently redirects the old vercel.app address, so links shared before the move still work.
  • Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools: verify the site and submit the sitemap. Bing also powers ChatGPT search and Copilot, and our IndexNow script asks it to re-crawl after each deploy.
  • Launch day: turn on the Product Hunt badge in the hero, and collect links from Product Hunt, Reddit (r/languagelearning), Indie Hackers and Hacker News.
  • After launch: re-check the preview cards in LinkedIn Post Inspector and the Facebook Sharing Debugger, and add real testimonials.
